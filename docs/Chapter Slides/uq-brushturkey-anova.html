Reference: Eiby, Y. A., & Booth, D. T. (2009). The effects of incubation temperature on the morphology and composition of Australian Brush-turkey (Alectura lathami) chicks. Journal of Comparative Physiology B, 179, 875–882. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00360-009-0370-4

Data provenance: be transparent

  • The paper reports group means and standard errors, not original per-egg observations.
  • The CSV contains reconstructed whole-day incubation periods matching the published summaries.
  • The analysis uses n = 28 because the published test is F(2,25), so N = 25 + 3.
This is useful teaching material: published degrees of freedom reveal the analysis sample size.

The ANOVA structure

Response

Incubation period in days.

Factor

Incubation temperature.

Levels

32 °C, 34 °C, 36 °C.

The model is

incubation period = overall mean + temperature effect + residual error

The omnibus ANOVA test

The null hypothesis is that all three population means are equal. The alternative is that at least one mean differs.

From significance to meaning

  • A significant F-test says that not all means are equal.
  • It does not say which temperatures differ.
  • We use a post hoc procedure to compare all pairs while controlling multiplicity.

Teaching takeaway

The colder 32 °C condition is associated with a much longer incubation period, while the 34 °C and 36 °C groups are closer. The post hoc comparison is what turns the omnibus result into a biological interpretation.
